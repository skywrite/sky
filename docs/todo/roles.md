# Reusable roles for expertise and ongoing responsibility

A Role gives Sky a durable professional remit, working methods, personality, and memory.
A person could create a GTM specialist, COO, or brand director once and give it different assignments over time.
The role carries the expertise; a workstream carries an objective, its activities, decisions, and results.

The intended value is continuity: less repeated explanation, stronger judgment, and useful work that keeps moving.
A role should learn how to work with the person and bring them the decisions that need their attention.
For someone developing a skill, it should also help them become better at the work.

## Roles and workstreams

| Concept | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Role | A reusable remit, professional approach, working style, and relevant accumulated knowledge. |
| Skill | A method for a particular kind of work, supported by references, examples, tools, and quality standards. |
| Workstream | The intended outcome, current understanding, activities, decisions, source material, and results. |
| Assignment | The role's responsibility in a particular context, including scope and authority. |
| Personality | How the role interacts with the person: direct, exploratory, encouraging, methodical, or challenging. |

A role can contribute to several workstreams, and a workstream can draw on several roles.
The same GTM specialist could advise on one launch and drive agreed experiments for another.
Its assignments would retain their own objectives, context, and permissions.

A COO may need a view across workstreams to identify competing demands, shared dependencies, and missing ownership.
That broader remit should help the person coordinate their work while keeping each activity and result in its owning
workstream.
Specialist contributions should return to the assignment that requested them so the person can follow the work in
one place.

## What a role knows and does

A useful role brief would establish:

- **Remit:** What the person relies on it for and what good work means.
- **Methods:** Relevant skills, curated references, examples, critique criteria, and available tools.
- **Working style:** How it communicates, challenges assumptions, explains tradeoffs, and collaborates.
- **Knowledge:** Relevant facts about the person's preferences, business, audience, and existing work.
- **Learning:** Lessons supported by feedback or observed results, with their evidence and scope preserved.

Expertise should be demonstrated through the quality of the work and the ability to explain its reasoning.
Personality shapes the relationship; it does not establish competence or execution authority.
A role acting as a critical editor should be able to identify a weak claim, explain the problem, and help improve it.

An assignment would establish the outcome, relevant sources, expected contribution, cadence, and permitted actions.
The person could ask the role to coach them, work alongside them, or handle an agreed responsibility.
Those choices can vary by activity: coaching a presentation while handling its research and preparation, for example.

Continuing responsibility should let Sky resume useful work, notice missing inputs, and surface consequential choices.
Waiting for an input is a valid state; each review should reflect what changed and what can now advance.
Existing execution boundaries still apply, including the distinction between preparing an artifact and achieving an
external result.

## Start with a role before organizing work

Someone should be able to get value from a role before they actively use workstreams.
An entry point could be: "Act as my COO and help me understand what needs my attention."
Sky would clarify the most useful responsibility, propose a concise role brief, and begin working with the person.

As ongoing objectives emerge, Sky could suggest organizing them into workstreams and carrying the relevant context
forward.
Creating a role should not require defining an organization chart, configuring every skill, or planning every
assignment.
The brief should remain inspectable and editable as actual work reveals what helps.

Conversation, ongoing assignments, and workstream views should make the same role accessible in the context where it is
needed.
Its place in navigation and the details of the creation flow remain open design questions.
Any interface should extend Sky's existing shell and controls.

## Example roles

| Role | Example responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| GTM specialist | Develop positioning, identify audiences, and plan and assess launch experiments. |
| COO | Coordinate execution, identify bottlenecks, and track commitments across workstreams. |
| Brand director | Develop positioning and voice, maintain a content practice, and coach the person's judgment. |

A brand director illustrates the depth this would require.
It would develop an understanding of the intended audience, desired reputation, credible experience, preferred voice,
and what material can be used publicly.
It could support content for X, LinkedIn, Instagram, and TikTok, with an approach suited to each medium.

The recurring practice would connect:

1. Capture ideas through conversation and selected source material.
2. Develop a clear point, supporting examples, and an appropriate treatment.
3. Produce drafts or media, critique them against explicit standards, and revise.
4. Bring the person the review, decisions, or recording that require their participation.
5. Publish through available capabilities and the assignment's explicit authority.
6. Observe results, assess the experiment, and use the evidence to guide the next cycle.

A script, finished video, and published post each need distinct completion evidence.
Production, publication, and performance measurement depend on capabilities that must be available to the assignment.

Coaching belongs in this practice too.
The role could draw out stronger ideas through interviews, explain edits, give focused exercises, and track
improvements.
Success includes both useful output and the person's growing ability to judge and create it.

## Learning from feedback and results

An edit that makes a draft sound more like its author and a post that attracts the intended audience provide different
kinds of evidence.
Preserve both without treating a single successful example as a universal rule.
The person should be able to inspect, correct, and retire lessons.

Keep facts and results in their originating conversation or assignment, linked to the workstream when one exists.
Reusable lessons need an explicit scope so a preference or discovery from one context does not silently govern another.
Personal writing should continue to use the shared [Writing Voice](../../src/lib/writingVoice/docs/README.md), including
its existing path for learning from the person's edits and accepted revisions.
Role personality should govern how Sky collaborates; the owner's voice should govern writing in their name.

## Existing foundations and proposed first step

[Workstreams](../topics/workstreams/README.md) already provide ongoing assistance, delegated decisions,
deliverable checks, and durable records of work.
Writing Voice provides a shared writer and learning from edits.
Roles would connect reusable expertise and responsibility to those foundations.

The current workstream executor prepares written deliverables and communication drafts, with separately scoped report
delivery.
The broader brand practice would need research, media production, publishing, and outcome measurement connected to its
assignments.

A candidate first step is one Brand Director role completing a useful weekly cycle on one channel, including feedback
and observed results.
That experience would help establish what should be shared by other roles.
The first role and responsibility still need to be chosen; a concrete COO or GTM responsibility may be a better starting
point if it offers more immediate value.

Judge progress by reduced repeated direction, stronger work, appropriate initiative, and useful learning.
For coaching assignments, also assess whether the person develops better judgment and needs less help over time.

## Questions to resolve before implementation

- What specific responsibility should the first role take on, and what evidence would show that it is doing it well?
- How should someone move between talking with a role and reviewing its ongoing assignments?
- Which lessons belong to the role, the person, a business context, or a single workstream?
- How should roles coordinate when their recommendations or assignments conflict?
- What should a COO see and coordinate across workstreams, and which decisions should return to the person?
