# Connected outcomes can advance in parallel

Status: rationale for the proposal and interactive mockup. All examples are
synthetic; the current contract lives in [README.md](README.md).

The earlier examples showed shared dependencies but did not show how several
independent outcomes can contribute to one effort. Treating every connection
as a prerequisite would make a launch plan appear to wait for entire contracts
and the whole product. It would also turn the design into a nested task tree.

The new scenario uses four peer workstreams: a pilot-ready stablecoin experience,
an accepted GTM plan, a revised agency agreement, and an advisor agreement. They
support a shared sports-partnership goal. Product and GTM inform each other;
the two agreements strengthen the GTM effort and the shared goal.

A contribution records why related work matters. A required result records what
one specific action needs. In the mock, assigning campaign responsibilities needs
the agreed agency scope, while audience and channel decisions remain available.
Confirming that scope makes the consuming action ready before the agency’s
amendment is signed. Finishing the agreement does not close GTM or the product.

The page retains one primary next action and compact connected-work rows. The
full map is optional and clickable, with contribution links distinct from the
specific required input. The mock reconciles action readiness from confirmed
criteria without copying results into other workstreams’ completion criteria.
Its relationship fields remain illustrative until persistence is designed.
