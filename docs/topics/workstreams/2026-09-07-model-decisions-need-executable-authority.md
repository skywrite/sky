# Model decisions need executable authority and real-provider verification

The first workstream runner could prepare proposals and artifacts but could not
resolve a delegated decision or establish that a requested local deliverable was
complete. That left otherwise authorized work waiting for another owner action.

Decision responsibility now belongs to the external workstream grant, keyed by
the exact decision identity. Human and recommendation modes retain human
resolution. Delegation declares permitted options, the question being decided,
risk and monetary limits, required sources and stakeholders, assumptions, and
expiry. The model provides an assessment; a deterministic controller checks the
current grant and evidence before recording a choice. Workstream drive mode alone
does not delegate a human decision.

Evidence quotes must occur in current selected sources. Stakeholder evidence
needs source attribution established outside the model. Unconfirmed assumptions
remain unconfirmed even when the owner expressly allows using them; rejected,
stale, missing, or contradictory material produces an escalation. Model
confidence cannot substitute for authority or evidence. A decision records its
question, choice, rationale, source versions, assumption state, actor, run, and
grant revision. Canonical decision documents preserve that attribution across
restarts and reject retrospective replacement of a delegated result.

Local deliverables use an independent model assessment against the owner's exact
success criteria, followed by deterministic quotation and required-source checks.
A complete local draft can satisfy a delegated drafting activity. It cannot
establish sending, approval, signing, recording, or another external result.

An actual provider trial exposed a failure that deterministic fixtures could
not: the combined workstream review and decision schema exceeded the provider's
compiled structured-output grammar limit. Removing numeric and length bounds
did not make that combined grammar acceptable. The complex draft and review request
JSON text and validates the entire original Zod schema locally before the
controller can act. Smaller report and independent-assessment calls keep
structured generation with structural provider schemas; the original local
schemas still enforce all content limits. Invalid responses remain failures.

A synthetic notebook trial then exercised two real model turns: a scoped cohort
decision resolved from attributed input, followed by a generated Markdown scope
that passed an independent model assessment and became done. The trial used an
isolated notebook and state directory, without communication callbacks or live
notebook mutations. Deterministic tests additionally cover policy boundaries,
source and assumption integrity, canonical attribution, and atomic policy-route
updates. Sending and reporting transport have their own verification boundaries.
