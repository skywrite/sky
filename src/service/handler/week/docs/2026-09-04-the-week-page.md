# 2026-09-04 — the week page

It was Friday morning and the web page said Thursday. The notebook's day
is the last started day, and Friday had not been started; nothing on the
page said so, and nothing on it could start the day. The first sketch was
a card on the day page listing the day to start and the days never ended.
It did not feel right: those are facts about the week, and the week is the
notebook's own unit — the directory, the seven day files, the plan written
on Sunday, the check-ins graded against it.

So the week became a page. Its days say what they are in a word or two,
and the one waiting to be started carries the page's one primary button.
The plan sits under them with the latest check-in's word on each goal, and
the check-in itself below that.

Two questions came up on the way. First, how to know a new day is due.
Comparing the notebook's day to the machine's date breaks the moment a
plane lands: the machine changes zones, the day file keeps the zone it
started in. The notebook clock already does the right sum — it converts
now into the started day's zone and counts hours past 24 — so the page
asks it and nothing else. Second, where next week's things go before next
week has files. The plumbing was already there: week:plan's Week-Next
queue in the next files, and the dated lists in the schedule files that
land on a day when it starts. The Next week page shows both and adds to
both, and shows the backlog beside them, so rounding out a week is a
matter of dropping things in.

Two things were left where they were. The clock's one travel defect —
flying west after starting a day in a zone far ahead, where the walk back
from the machine's date misses the started day — is parked. And week:new
stays a hand step, Sunday or Monday, now one button; automating it is for
later.
